## D. Challenge (relevant lenses only)

### D.1 Problem and use-case validity

- **Hypothesis (leading candidate):** the unmet job is *background AI agents that perform multi-step work and proactively return results/asks to the user.* This is a genuinely current and interesting frontier where web limitations are felt (agents that die when the tab closes; no cheap proactive channel beyond push+notification).
- **Contradiction to resolve:** "background work" and "re-engagement" are different jobs with different best-fit layers. Background *work* for agents is increasingly done **server-side** (queues, scheduled tasks, cloud agents) and surfaced through the *existing* push/notification surface. The browser may not need to own the work at all.
- **Recommendation:** Decompose into at least three job clusters and validate each independently: (1) deferred/scheduled work (today: Background Sync, Periodic Background Sync, server queues); (2) long-running transfers (today: Background Fetch); (3) proactive user re-engagement (today: Push + Notifications + Badging). Map each cluster's current web baseline and its gaps *before* proposing anything new.

### D.2 Existing-web alternatives (the gap the team skipped) — facts to verify, not findings

These capabilities **exist** today (fact as to existence/purpose). Their **current cross-engine support, defaults, and restrictions must be retrieved and dated by the team** (unknowns).

- **Service Workers** — the foundational background/event primitive; persists across navigations; independent of page; the substrate for push, sync, fetch, notifications. *(Fact: exists. Support/positions: verify.)*
- **Push API + Web Push (notifications)** — server-originated wake-up of the service worker; the primary re-engagement channel. *(Fact: exists; WebKit shipped web push for installed web apps on macOS and iOS/iPadOS in recent years — verify current status.)*
- **Notifications API** — the visible re-engagement surface.
- **Background Sync API** — defers work until connectivity returns (`sync` event). *(Fact: exists; Chromium-origin — verify others.)*
- **Periodic Background Sync API** — service worker wakes on a schedule to refresh content; restricted to installed PWAs. *(Fact: exists; Chromium-origin — this is the closest existing primitive to "scheduled background work," and its **restricted scope and single-engine history are themselves the most important prior art** to study.)*
- **Background Fetch API** — long downloads/uploads surviving page/browser close, with user-visible progress. *(Fact: exists; Chromium-origin — verify.)*
- **Badging API** — app-icon badge for subtle re-engagement without a full notification. *(Fact: exists; WICG — verify support.)*
- **Wake Lock API, Page Lifecycle API, Web Locks, IndexedDB, Cache Storage, OPFS, Broadcast Channel, SharedWorker, `requestIdleCallback`** — supporting primitives for persistence, coordination, and opportunistic work. *(Facts: exist.)*
- **Known deliberate restrictions (verify current state):** push delivery historically required a visible notification (`userVisibleOnly`) to prevent silent background activity; "silent push" has been a long-running, contested topic. This restriction is **itself the policy decision** any new capability would have to revisit. *(Fact as to the existence of the debate; current policy: verify.)*

**Recommendation:** Build an "existing-web alternatives matrix" (capability × job × current support × known restriction × evidence source+date) as the first deliverable. If a team cannot show *why each of these is insufficient for the target job*, the proposal is premature.

### D.3 Adjacent-platform alternatives — research targets (not invented findings)

The request lists families the team has not studied. I list the **patterns to extract** from each; the team must retrieve current primary sources and record evidence classes. Per the skill: *extract the job, permission/control model, lifecycle, distribution, business incentives, user cost, failure modes, abuse, and interoperability consequences; then assess what must change to fit web principles.*

- **iOS/iPadOS (already studied, but in isolation):** background modes, `BGTaskScheduler`/background fetch, APNs, silent push, **Live Activities**, **Critical Alerts**, App Intents/Shortcuts. *Pattern to extract:* the OS aggressively budgets and schedules background time; permissions are explicit; silent/background activity is the exception, tightly rationed.
- **Android:** WorkManager, foreground services (with persistent notification — note the **user-visibility requirement**, directly analogous to the web's `userVisibleOnly` debate), JobScheduler, FCM, Doze/App Standby, notification channels. *Pattern to extract:* even native platforms force visible foreground-service notifications and impose background-execution limits precisely because unconstrained background work harms users.
- **WeChat / Alipay / Douyin mini-programs (China — unstudied):** subscribe-message (订阅消息) permission-gated re-engagement model that **replaced** earlier template/broadcast messaging; service notifications; the mini-program lifecycle and "background" model; the super-app distribution layer. *Pattern to extract:* these ecosystems **gated and permissioned re-engagement precisely because broadcast re-engagement was abused** — a directly relevant prior-art case for the web. *Note:* China has also regulated marketing messages (complaint/opt-out mechanisms); the abuse-and-regulation history is itself evidence.
- **Chat/messaging apps (unstudied):** bot platforms, embedded mini-apps, and **messaging-window rules** (e.g., 24-hour customer-service windows, template-message gating). *Pattern to extract:* mature platforms have already converged on **consent + windowing + gating** for proactive outreach — exactly the design space a web capability would enter.
- **LLM/agent platforms (unstudied, likely the real motivator):** background/scheduled agents that perform multi-step work and re-engage users via push/email/chat; on-device vs. cloud agent runtimes; agent "proactive messaging." *Pattern to extract:* this is where background-work + re-engagement is the core UX, and where the web's tab-limited, no-cheap-proactive-channel model is most felt. **But note the contradiction:** most of this work already runs server-side and reaches users through existing channels — so the marginal browser-layer need must be proven, not assumed.
- **Framework/server solutions (unstudied):** the empirical reality that **most background work + re-engagement is already a server job** — cron, task queues (Cloud Tasks/SQS/etc.), push services, email, SMS, webhooks, server-side scheduling — with the browser providing only the thin re-engagement surface (push/notifications) that already exists. *Pattern to extract:* "apps do background work" is usually "servers do background work." The browser-layer question is narrow and specific.

### D.4 "Why not" — the strongest case against adding a browser API (the requested lens)

These are **principle-grounded inferences and hypotheses**, not recorded positions of any party (which must be retrieved). I steelman, per the skill; I do not choose weak objections.

1. **The web's conservative background model is a feature, not a gap.** Unconstrained background execution is the surface that produces covert tracking, battery drain, metered-data cost, and attention manipulation. Other engines and civil-society actors have historically scrutinized exactly this surface. "iOS/Android can" may reflect native's different trust/permission model, not a web deficiency.
2. **Re-engagement is values-laden, not just a capability gap.** Making proactive re-engagement cheaper/easier lowers the cost of spam, dark patterns, and compulsive-use design. The **user** may not want "more re-engagement"; the **site** does. The skill requires separating these.
3. **The browser may be the wrong layer.** If the job is already well-served server-side (and it largely is), a browser API risks **ossification** and Chrome-only lock-in for marginal benefit.
4. **Interoperability reality.** Background-sync/periodic-sync/push have a long history of **single-engine or incomplete cross-engine** support. A new capability that ships Chrome-only undermines Baseline and the cross-engine promise, and hands other engines a legitimate "we chose differently on purpose" critique.
5. **Copying native lifecycle without adaptation** (explicit skill anti-pattern). Native background models assume installed, permissioned, OS-managed apps with install-time trust. The web has an ambient, per-origin, no-install trust model. The primitives do not transfer directly.
6. **Mini-app and chat precedents argue for *gating*, not *more capability*.** The relevant lesson from WeChat/Alipay/messaging platforms is that **mature platforms added friction** (subscribe consent, messaging windows) to control re-engagement abuse — the opposite of "make background outreach easier."
7. **User cost asymmetry.** The site captures the engagement benefit; the user pays in battery, data, memory, thermal load, attention, and privacy. On low-end devices and metered networks — where most of the next billion users are — this asymmetry is sharpest.

**Strongest case *for* a capability (to be fair):** the rise of **background AI agents** may create a genuinely new, previously-rare job (long-horizon agent work that needs to persist beyond a tab and reach back to the user), for which the current push+notification+service-worker stack is awkward, and where a **well-gated, user-consented, privacy-respecting** primitive could add real value *if* designed with the abuse model first. This is a **hypothesis** that needs evidence, not a conclusion.

- **Principle-grounded inferences (to verify against current primary sources):** Mozilla's published web-vision/standards-positions and WebKit's standards-positions and tracking-prevention policy, the W3C Ethical Web Principles / Design Principles (priority of constituencies; "safety when visiting a page") / Privacy Principles, and EFF interoperability/privacy materials are the **canonical starting points to retrieve and date**. ### D.6 User-impact and cost rehearsal (pre-review, not approval)

Separate the beneficiaries and cost-bearers explicitly:

- **End-user benefit (hypothesized, unproven):** agents/apps complete work and notify conveniently; content is fresh on return.
- **End-user cost (concrete, must be measured):** battery; **metered-data**; memory/thermal; notification fatigue and compulsive-use risk; **covert background activity** (privacy); accessibility (notifications for AT users, reduced-motion/cognitive load); low-end-device slowdown; enterprise/managed-device and policy impact; **who pays** for the background compute.
- **Developer/site benefit:** engagement/retention.
- **Implementer cost:** new lifecycle, permissions UI, abuse/anti-fraud surface, support burden, cross-engine divergence.
- **Recommendation:** treat user-impact as **load-bearing** for this category, not a late-stage check. The dimension that most often blocks background/re-engagement features is user cost + abuse, not developer ergonomics.
