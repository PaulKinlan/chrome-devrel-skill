## 4. Challenge (relevant lenses)

- **Evidence classification lens (primary).** Public workarounds prove a *job* exists, not that the org wants *this* API. Inference from "they wrote a polyfill" to "they want the platform to ship X" is a hypothesis, not a commitment.
- **"Why not?" / adversarial lens.** A workaround can mean: (a) the platform is missing something (supports the API), or (b) the task is rare/niche, or (c) a library already solves it well enough, or (d) the browser is the wrong layer, or (e) the workarounds are *against* a native API existing (e.g., for privacy reasons). The shortlist must record which interpretation each signal supports, including counterevidence.
- **Ecosystem research lens.** Demand evidence must come from *multiple independent source families* (framework trackers, Stack Overflow, native/mini-app equivalents, conference talks, support portals, non-English communities), not a single repo. One library with 10k stars is not 10k demand signals — it is one maintainer's choice plus weak usage context.
- **Privacy/consent lens.** Building a contact list from public profiles and naming organizations without consent has real cost. Outreach needs an approved purpose and a handling plan (per `modules/ecosystem-and-customer-research.md` §2/§6).
- **Attribution/legal lens (`unknown`, route to counsel).** Naming a company in a public "demand" list can imply endorsement. Whether that's permissible depends on jurisdiction and the company's own policies; this is a routing question for Legal, not a DevRel conclusion.
