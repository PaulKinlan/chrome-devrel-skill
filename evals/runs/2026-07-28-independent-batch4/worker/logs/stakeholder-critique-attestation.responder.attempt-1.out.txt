## 3. Challenge

### 3a. Power and capability change

- **New capability:** a site can obtain a signal — likely cryptographic — attesting that the client (browser/OS/device) is in a set the site or a delegated attester considers "trusted."
- **Gains power/control:** large sites/fraud teams, adtech/DRM-adjacent use cases, the **attester** (whoever signs or validates the trust list — typically the browser vendor, OS vendor, or a delegated cloud service), and device/OS vendors whose clients are "trusted" by default.
- **Loses choice/access:** users of non-mainstream, modified, privacy-hardened, rooted/custom-OS, older, low-end, or region-restricted devices; users who depend on **assistive technology or automation**; **independent and open-source browsers**; automation/testing/archival/research (web archives, crawlers, search); people who don't control their device (shared, enterprise, locked-down).
- **Benign site:** throttle bots, cut fraud, gate premium/paid flows, raise friction for abuse.
- **Hostile or dominant site:** exclude competing browsers and privacy-respecting clients, force-disable ad/tracking blockers, enforce DRM/tie-in, price-discriminate, coerce behavior, entrench incumbent distribution, exclude users whose devices can't pass.
- **One browser alone:** creates a de-facto trust signal controlled by one vendor; rival browsers' users begin failing checks; sites integrate around it; fait accompli before interop.
- **Every browser ships:** still concentrates "trust" at device/OS/browser layer; hardware-backed attestation still excludes open-source/custom implementations; a two-tier "trusted vs. untrusted" web crystallizes.
- **User detect/deny/revoke/audit/workaround:** *largely no* — the entire value proposition is that users *cannot* forge or bypass it. That asymmetry between site capability and user agency is the core risk.
- **Irreversible effects before interop:** sites integrate a single-vendor signal; users get conditioned to degraded access; other browsers face a take-it-or-lose-access choice.

### 3b. Stakeholder map (retention rationale)

**Affected end users (split, because "users" is not one group):**
- Mainstream users on default devices — *may* gain fraud reduction.
- Privacy-conscious, open-source-OS, rooted/custom-ROM, and Tor/anonymity users — *likely lose access or are fingerprinted*.
- Low-end / old-device / metered-network / region-restricted users — *risk exclusion by attestation failure*.
- **Accessibility-software users** — high-risk: AT that injects events, reads/modifies the accessibility tree, or drives the browser may be classified as a modification.
- Users without device control (shared, enterprise, abusive household) — coercion concern.
- Automation/testing/archival/research users (incl. Internet Archive, search crawlers, academic scraping) — breakage/exclusion.

**Implementers:** Mozilla/Firefox, WebKit/Safari, Brave, Vivaldi, Opera, downstream Chromium embedders, Tor Browser. Their *independent implementability* and market survival are directly at stake.

**Standards/governance:** W3C TAG, WICG, WHATWG; possibly IETF if attestation/crypto is involved. *Open question:* is there a standards home at all?

**Civil society & researchers:** digital-rights organizations (EFF and peers), privacy/security/fraud researchers, accessibility organizations, competition/DP-focused NGOs.

**Regulators:** competition authorities (EU DMA, UK CMA, US FTC/DOJ and peers), data-protection authorities (GDPR DPAs and peers), consumer-protection and accessibility regulators. *Routed to qualified counsel; no conclusions drawn.*

**Industry:** payments, streaming/DRM, ticketing, gaming, social platforms, adtech/publishers, identity providers, device/secure-enclave vendors, potential attesters.

**Open-source/firmware communities:** custom ROM, Linux mobile, independent engine contributors.

### 3d. Critique matrix — material themes

Each theme: steelmanned concern → severity (S) / likelihood (L) → current response → gap → next action.

1. **Two-tier web / exclusion.** *"A capability whose purpose is to discriminate will, at scale and under incentive, be used to exclude clients the site prefers not to serve — non-mainstream browsers, privacy tools, AT, low-end devices. The web's open-access guarantee erodes."* S: high · L: high. Response: non-binding guideline "sites should not exclude browsers." **Gap: guideline is not enforceable; incentives point the other way.** Action: design an *enforceable or structurally-bounded* scope; demonstrate fallback parity.
2. **Self-preferencing / competition.** *"The attester (often the browser vendor) decides what is 'trusted' while its own browser is in the trust set. That is gatekeeping and, in several jurisdictions, the kind of self-preferencing competition law targets."* S: high · L: medium-high. Response: unknown. **Gap: no governance/audit design; no separation of attester from browser vendor interest.** Action: legal routing; governance design that removes single-vendor control; multi-attester model.
3. **Privacy / fingerprinting.** *"Attestation tokens, even short-lived, add a correlation vector across origins and time; the attestation handshake is itself a tracking surface."* S: medium-high · L: medium-high. Response: presumed "unlinkable token." **Gap: unlinkability unproven; no differential-privacy/fingerprinting analysis.** Action: formal privacy analysis; short-lived, origin-bound, blinded-token design with public proofs.
4. **End-user agency, consent, refusal.** *"Users can't meaningfully consent when refusal means being blocked, and they can't forge/audit the signal — agency is one-directional."* S: high · L: high. Response: unknown. **Gap: no user-facing disclosure, choice, appeal, or fallback design.** Action: UX + policy for refusal that preserves access to a meaningful fallback.
5. **Open-source / independent implementability.** *"Hardware-rooted attestation cannot be independently implemented by an open-source browser or a custom OS; the API structurally excludes them."* S: high · L: high (if HW-rooted). Response: unknown. **Gap: no independent-implementability proof.** Action: design that is royalty-free and implementable without proprietary hardware or vendor keys.
6. **Accessibility.** *"AT and input methods that read/modify the accessibility tree or drive the browser may register as 'untrusted modification,' degrading or denying access to disabled users."* S: high · L: medium. Response: unknown. **Gap: no AT-compatibility testing; W3C priority-of-constituencies not addressed.** Action: AT compatibility plan; guarantee AT never reduces trust.
7. **Automation / testing / archival / research / search.** *"Crawlers, archives, test infrastructure, and search indexers cannot attest and get degraded or blocked — damaging the recordable, searchable web."* S: medium-high · L: high. Response: unknown. **Gap: no machine/non-human-access path.** Action: documented, non-abusable path for archivists and search.
8. **Security monoculture, coercion, authoritarian misuse.** *"'Trusted client' becomes a coercion instrument — authoritarian regimes, domestic-abuse control, mandatory-attestation mandates. A monoculture of 'genuine' clients also reduces defensive diversity."* S: high · L: medium. Response: unknown. **Gap: no abuse-at-scale / second-order analysis.** Action: misuse-at-scale threat model; sunset/reversibility design.
9. **Scope creep (DRM → identity → gating).** *"'Fraud' is the entry use case; the same primitive enables DRM, identity binding, and content/behavior gating — each step expanding who is excluded."* S: high · L: high. Response: unknown. **Gap: no enforced scope limits or sunset.** Action: explicit, narrow, sunset-bound scope with compliance signals.
10. **The non-enforceable promise (central contradiction).** *"Offering a discrimination capability while promising it won't be used to discriminate is internally contradictory; a reviewer, journalist, or regulator will say so immediately."* S: high · L: certain. Response: stated guideline. **Gap: promise is unenforceable.** Action: replace promise with *structure* (bounded scope, enforceable signals, fallback parity) or narrow the capability.

**Strongest case *for* the proposal:** fraud, ATO, scalping, ad fraud, payment fraud, and severe abuse (including child-safety) are real and cause real user harm; sites have a legitimate interest in distinguishing abusive clients; native platforms already have integrity signals the web lacks; privacy-preserving (short-lived, unlinkable) attestation is an active research area and *could* in principle be done responsibly.

**Strongest case *against*:** it inserts a centralized, hard-to-audit, hard-to-independently-implement discrimination primitive into the open web, whose incentives predictably push toward browser exclusion and a two-tier web — and it does so while asking us to trust a non-enforceable promise. The harm is structural and asymmetric (sites gain a capability; users lose agency they cannot recover).

**Conditions under which a skeptical stakeholder might shift:** multi-implementer governance with **no single vendor** controlling the trust list; hardware-agnostic, royalty-free, independently implementable by open-source browsers; cryptographically demonstrated unlinkability; a **real, parity-preserving user fallback**; an auditable, appeal-able misuse regime with enforcement teeth; narrow, sunset-bound scope; published threat + abuse-at-scale model; multi-engine shipping commitment; no dependence on one vendor's cloud/identity/keys.

**Redesign triggers (each forces redesign or narrower scope):** any design returning a durable/identifiable/vendor-bound signal; any design with no user fallback; any design open-source browsers cannot independently implement; any signal sites could use to exclude browsers/AT/extensions; evidence that launch partners intend to gate access.

**Delay / abandon triggers:** no cross-engine support; evidence of intended access-gating; inability to prove unlinkability; regulator objection; no appeal path.

### 3e. Announcement red-team (apply *before* anything public)

- **Overstates demand/commitment?** Risk: yes, if "fraud is a big problem" is presented as "developers demand *this API*." (A4 pattern — survey/category interest ≠ API demand.)
- **Hides known negative positions?** Near-certain to exist post-WEI-precedent; must be surfaced, not buried.
- **Site/Chrome benefit dressed as user benefit?** "Lower fraud" is a *site* benefit; the *user* benefit ("you'll see less fraud") is asserted and unproven — and users may experience it as *less access and more lock-in*.
- **Ignores who pays?** Users pay in agency/privacy/access; open-source browsers pay in survival; archivists/search pay in breakage. Must be disclosed.
- **Chrome-only framed as a web standard?** Forbidden framing. Until multi-engine, this is a *proposal*, not "the web platform."
- **Safeguard promises without analyzing bypass incentives?** The "sites should not exclude browsers" promise is exactly this — a safeguard the mechanism's own incentives erode.
- **Delight demos hiding constraints?** A fraud-block demo hides exclusion, AT breakage, privacy cost, fallback gaps.
- **Scope/naming/launch intent not actually committed?** Must not announce shipping intent the eng/review team hasn't committed to.

### A. Strategic framing (provisional)
- **Do not announce a capability.** Public posture = *"we are exploring a hard problem and asking for input; here is what we're worried about getting wrong."* This is the only framing consistent with the evidence state and the nearest precedent.
- **Lead with the contradiction, don't hide it.** State plainly: this primitive's value is discrimination; we know that is exactly what makes it dangerous; here is the *structural* (not merely promised) scope that makes browser/AT/extension exclusion infeasible — or we have no announcement.
- **Separate user benefit from site benefit.** Never say "users benefit from lower fraud" without the qualifier and the evidence; disclose who bears the cost.
- **No "the web now has X" framing** until multi-engine. It is a *proposal under incubation*.

### B. Positioning statements (each tied to evidence that does not yet exist — do not publish until sourced)
1. *Problem:* "Abuse, fraud, and automation cause real harm to users and sites." — *Fact (general); proposal-specific quantification = Unknown.*

4. *Governance:* "Trust decisions are not controlled by a single vendor." — *Design requirement, currently unmet (Unknown).*
5. *Fallback:* "Declining attestation always preserves meaningful access." — *Design requirement, currently unmet (Unknown).*
