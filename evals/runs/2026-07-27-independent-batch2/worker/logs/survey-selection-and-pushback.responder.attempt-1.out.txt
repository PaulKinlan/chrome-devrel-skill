### 3.3 Adversarial "why not?" (selected, strongest cases)

- **The need is rare or already solved.** A library, a server-side model, or a native/OS API may already cover the job. *Evidence that would rebut:* attributable developer reports of an unmet, recurring, costly job that only on-device browser integration solves. **Status: unknown.**
- **The browser is the wrong layer / premature ossification.** Shipping an on-device AI API can freeze a fast-moving model/tooling ecosystem into a lowest-common-denominator primitive. *Evidence that would rebut:* a primitive that's low-level enough not to ossify, with a credible Baseline/interop path. **Status: unknown.**
- **Partners want the *outcome*, not *this API*.** "Interested in AI" ≠ "want this surface." **Status: partial/contradicted by the survey's own scope.**
- **Demand is manufactured by selection bias or one vendor ecosystem.** *Evidence that would rebut:* demand across independent source families, regions, and frameworks. **Status: unknown.**
- **On-device cost is borne by users/low-end devices, not the developers asking for it.** Classic "user is not the person bearing cost" mismatch. **Status: unknown — and the survey notably didn't probe it.**

**Recommendation on direction:** This is not a stop recommendation — there isn't evidence to stop, just as there isn't evidence to proceed to trial. The defensible posture is **incubation with genuine co-design**: keep the explainer honest, retract the overclaim, and run real research. If, after representative research, the falsification cases hold, the right moves are narrow, park, or stop — not push.
