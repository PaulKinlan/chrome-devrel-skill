## 3. Challenge (relevant lenses)

**A. Problem & use-case validity** *(primary)*
- *Contradiction:* the PM's "ready" claim is contradicted by the evidence base — almost every input the transition packet requires is absent. That is an internal contradiction (claim vs. evidence), not yet a contradicted external finding.
- *Hypothesis:* each of the three use cases is a **hypothesis** ("X happens, costs Y, no current solution"), not a fact.
- *Resolve via:* structured customer interviews (last-time-it-happened prompts), plus public signal in issue trackers / Stack Overflow / framework repos. Evidence ladder target for prototype entry: at least **observed public need + contacted-with-permission + interest** for the lead use case.

**B. Ecosystem & customer research (demand, workarounds, frameworks, adjacent platforms, counterevidence)** — see `modules/ecosystem-and-customer-research.md`.
- *Unknowns:* demand; current workarounds and their cost; framework/server behavior; native/mini-app/chat/LLM-agent equivalents.
- *Resolve via:* a bounded research sprint with a query matrix, a source/community map, an evidence ledger, and an explicit **"why not?"** (falsification) pass. Record saturation; never equate search volume with evidence quality.

**C. "Why not?" / adversarial test** *(mandatory before transition)*
- *Hypotheses to test:* the need is rare/low-cost/already-solved; the person bearing cost isn't the proposed user; a library/framework/server/native pattern suffices; the browser is the wrong layer or would ossify a premature design; other engines have reason not to implement; the cost (privacy/security/a11y/competition/user agency) exceeds value; it only works on flagship/one region; partners want an outcome but not *this* API; "demand" is promotion/selection bias.
- *Recommendation:* write the strongest case **against** and the evidence that would rebut it, and what result would trigger continue / change / narrow / park / stop.

**D. Trial/partner candidate design**
- *Unknown:* no candidate trial users.
- *Resolve via:* **segment-balanced candidate hypotheses** (not endorsements), an outreach plan with consent/attribution choices, and interview prompts focused on past behavior and cost — not "do you like it?"

**E. Prototype learning goals & measurement**
- *Unknown:* no learning questions, success criteria, or stop/redesign conditions.
- *Recommendation:* a prototype with no learning goals is the #1 incubation→prototype failure mode. Define the specific uncertainty the build resolves and what result triggers each next step.

**F. Alternatives analysis (explainer-level)**
- *Unknown:* no rejected-with-evidence alternatives.
- *Recommendation:* the explainer's "Alternatives considered" must be evidence-backed, not gesture-y; otherwise the motivation is unsupported.

**G. Interoperability & standards posture (early)**
- *Unknown:* no venue/other-engine signals.
- *Recommendation:* at minimum identify the incubation venue and any prior art; don't present absence of objection as support.

**H. End-user impact + early review-rehearsal lenses**
- *Unknown:* end-user benefit/cost/control; privacy/security/a11y at the explainer level.
- *Recommendation:* open these in the explainer now (they're cheapest to influence early); full rehearsal belongs closer to wide review.

## 4. Plan (stage-appropriate: complete incubation, then reassess prototype)

**Outcome & exit criteria** — a defensible **incubation→prototype transition packet** containing: validated *or explicitly contested* jobs/use cases; segment & community map; current workarounds and costs; adjacent-platform pattern matrix; alternatives rejected with evidence; affected users/stakeholders and review risks; candidate partners with evidence stage; **prototype learning questions, environments, measures, and stop/redesign conditions**; explainer/source links.

**Evidence to collect**
- Representative customer/developer interviews (segment-balanced) for the three use cases.
- Workaround analysis across public issue trackers, Q&A, framework/library repos.
- Adjacent-platform comparison (native/mini-app/chat/LLM-agent as relevant), distilled to web-fit conclusions.
- Framework/server/build-tool signals (at research depth, not yet integration tests).
- Other-engine / standards signals and prior art.
- An explicit counterevidence ("why not?") brief.

**Activities & artifacts (DevRel-coordinated)**
- Ecosystem/customer research brief + evidence ledger + saturation statement.
- Interview/outreach plan + candidate map (consent-aware).
- "Why not?" brief.
- Explainer critique (goals/non-goals/alternatives/privacy-security-a11y/open questions).
- Prototype learning-goals & measurement one-pager.
- *Then,* only if criteria are met: a reviewed Intent to Prototype.

**Owners/reviewers (advisory role routing, incubation row)** — DevRel **coordinates** research; Product/Eng **contribute** API context & constraints; API Owner, Privacy, Security, Standards, A11y **consult / formal-review-if-required** per your canonical process; Comms not yet engaged.

**Dependencies & risks**
- Research may reveal the problem is rare, solved, or at the wrong layer → pivot/narrow/stop.
- Risk of locking API shape before integration understanding.
- Risk of promoting "interest" to "commitment" or naming non-consenting customers.
- If the team proceeds anyway: every public claim must stay at the evidence's actual rung.

**Suggested sequence** (DevRel-led, ~this order): 1) lock research questions + "why not"; 2) source/community map + query matrix; 3) interviews + public evidence; 4) alternatives/workaround + adjacent-platform matrix; 5) prototype learning goals; 6) transition-packet assembly; 7) reassess prototype readiness; 8) *if* ready, draft Intent to Prototype from the packet.
